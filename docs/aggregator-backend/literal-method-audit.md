## Verdict

**The package did not meet every literal step of the cited methods. The design-completeness gate remains open.** The fresh review found substantive defects that finite consistency checks had missed. Corrections below improve the candidate design; they do not fill absent textbook outputs, establish stakeholder agreement, execute the target service or prove deployed controls.

The [181-row inventory](methodology-steps.md) includes 171 source-linked activities, stages or guidelines and 10 local database review categories. Counts are coverage accounting, not independent tests or passed steps. The rows currently contain 55 documented artifacts, 88 partial dispositions, one missing-artifact gap, 26 deferred and 11 unassessed entries. A partial row can also contain a missing required output, such as the DDL specification. These categories must not be summed into a completion percentage.

## Three distinct checks

1. **Source-to-method check.** Reopen the actual primary sources, enumerate their selected activities, distinguish their literal steps from locally invented groupings, and compare each with recorded evidence. Paid ISO clauses and a full application-wide ASVS assessment were not available/performed; no conformity claim follows.
2. **Method-to-design check.** Swap reviewers away from their prior authoring areas. Independently examine requirements/procedures, data dependencies/interfaces, and security/transaction behavior; construct counterexamples and compare with pinned source. The primary reviewer reproduces source/contract contradictions before corrections.
3. **Correction-to-evidence check.** Review the changed contracts and readable mirrors, re-run finite model/source/trace checks, inspect the actual diff, and report absent execution separately. A checker passing is insufficient evidence that an omitted domain rule does not exist.

These are three different review passes with specialist agent assistance, not three human approvals, three full independent implementations, a facilitated workshop or a penetration test.

## Primary-method basis

The textbook's [database development chapter](https://opentextbc.ca/dbdesign01/chapter/chapter-13-database-development-process/) lists six lifecycle activities and five ER modelling guidelines. Its design output includes a DDL schema specification. The original DB-1 through DB-10 rows were our audit categories, not the textbook's literal list. The corrected inventory retains them transparently and adds the source's activities and [normalization stages](https://opentextbc.ca/dbdesign01/chapter/chapter-12-normalization/).

[NASA design solution definition](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/4-4-design-solution-definition/) tailors design output to lifecycle phase, so a paper design can be legitimate. Its verification/validation activities still require traceability, internal consistency and review against needs. That does not make a paper schema description the missing textbook DDL, or agent review stakeholder validation.

The official [SEI QAW third-edition report](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/documents/716/2003_005_001_14249.pdf) distinguishes stakeholder workshop activities from scenario artifacts. The [SEI ATAM method](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/library/atam-method-for-architecture-evaluation/) likewise includes participant evaluation. Those activities remain partial where only authored artifacts exist. The [NIST SSDF publication register](https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/ssdf/publications) still identifies version 1.1 as final and 1.2 as draft at this audit; the profile remains 1.1. The selected ASVS 5.0.0 references remain bounded changed-surface obligations, not a complete certification claim.
